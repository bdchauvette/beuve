# 0114. Design tokens, logical properties and diff color schemes

- Status: Accepted (overflow amended by [0118](0118-css-linting-with-eslint-css.md), default diff colors amended 2026-11-06, diff colors fixed by amendment 2026-11-06, themes kept out of settings by [0122](0122-themes-are-edited-in-the-source.md), attribution by [0119](0119-syntax-by-weight-and-slant.md)); amends [0025](0025-diff-cards-themes-and-lazy-mounting.md) (chrome and diff colors)
- Date: 2026-11-06

## Context

`index.css` had a flat list of hand-picked `--beuve-*` values, and components bypassed it in places: 24 raw `font-size: 11px`, a handful of 10, 15, 17 and 20 px sizes, `#fff` on accent buttons, five different shadows. Most UI text was 11 px (`--beuve-font-small`), which is hard to read, and from about 40 most readers have some presbyopia. Diff tints were 9–10% alpha lines and 20–22% changed words. Next to Alabaster's pale code background they nearly disappeared. Red and green is also the pair that red–green colour vision deficiency (about 1 in 12 men) confuses. It also casts removal as an error and addition as success, which is not what a reviewer means by either. Styles mixed physical (`margin-left`, `top`, `width`) and logical properties.

## Decision

- **Three tiers of tokens in `src/ui/index.css`.**
  - _Scales_ hold the primitives, named `--beuve-scale-<family>-<step>`. Colors come from Radix Colors 3.0.0 (MIT): slate for light gray, sage for dark gray (the Radix gray nearest Alabaster Dark's blue-green blacks), indigo for accent, plus green, red, amber, violet, blue and orange. Each is redefined under `prefers-color-scheme: dark`, and only the steps in use are listed. Semantic tokens follow Radix's step roles: 1–2 backgrounds, 3–5 element states, 6–8 borders, 9–10 solid fills, 11–12 text, `a`-steps for tints over another surface. Type, space and radius follow GitHub Primer (primer/primitives 11), in rem.
  - _Semantic_ tokens say what a value is for: `--beuve-bg`, `-surface`, `-surface-sunken`/`-hover`/`-pressed`, `-border`/`-border-strong`, `-text`/`-text-muted`/`-text-faint`, `-accent` (text and lines, step 11), `-accent-solid` with `-on-accent` (fills with white text, step 9), `-ok`/`-warn`/`-danger` with `-solid` variants, `-shadow-small`/`-large`, and the `--beuve-font-*` shorthands.
  - _Component_ tokens are the `--beuve-diff-*` set.
- **Larger type, nothing under 12 px.** The roles are caption 12 px (timestamps, badges, counts), small 13 px (dense rows, labels, the status bar; was 11), body 14 px (was 13; Primer's, Carbon's and Fluent's default), large 16, title 20, and code 13 px (was 12, and 12.5 in sheets) on a 20 px line. The card height estimate stays valid. 13 px is Primer's code size, used here for dense UI too. Windows 11 also sets 12 px regular as its minimum. The titlebar grows to 40 px and the status bar to 28. The sizes are in rem, so a later text-size preference only changes the root; View › Zoom already scales everything.
- **Diff tints step up as Primer's `diffBlob` tokens do**: line < gutter number < changed word, one Radix alpha step apart (green, red and blue a5/a6/a7; orange a4/a5/a6 and grey a6/a7/a8, so each pair reads at a similar weight). Bars are step 9, +/− counts step 11. The number cells get their own `--beuve-diff-*-number-bg`.
- **Three diff color schemes**, Settings › Diff › Colors, stored as `diffColors` in `settings.json` and validated in `display:set`. Every window sets the scheme as `data-diff-colors` on its root (`applyDiffColors`), and `index.css` swaps the `--beuve-diff-*` tokens:
  - `red-green` is the default and the convention.
  - `blue-orange`: added blue, removed orange. This is the pair GitHub's colorblind themes use, and it is distinguishable under the common colour vision deficiencies.
  - `neutral` ("Grey and blue"): removed grey, added blue. It reads as before and after rather than bad and good; JetBrains IDEs show deletions grey too.
    Review Board and Bitbucket use blue and yellow. That is a candidate if a fourth scheme is ever wanted.
- **Diagnostics get a tint as well as a squiggle.** `::highlight(beuve-diagnostic-error)` and `-warning` add a translucent background (`--beuve-danger-soft`, `--beuve-warn-soft`: Radix red and amber a4) under the wavy underline. A custom highlight's background composites over the line and changed-word backgrounds beneath it rather than replacing them, so a flagged token on an added line still reads as added. Information and hint stay underline-only, to keep the quieter severities quiet. A token the word diff counts as unchanged (a parameter name the old version also had) has no word background of its own; the gap is the library's, not the highlight's.
- **Logical properties.** Styles use `margin-inline-start`, `inset-block-start`, `inline-size`, `text-align: start` and the like. The CSS built as strings (`sheet-gutter-css-from.ts`, `insight-mark-css-from.ts`) does too. Box shorthands whose left and right agree (one to three values, or four with equal sides) are direction-safe and stay. Asymmetric ones become `*-block`/`*-inline`. The goal is a correct mirror under `dir="rtl"`, not vertical writing modes. Exceptions: `box-shadow` offsets (no logical form), and positions computed from `getBoundingClientRect()` (the hover card), which are physical viewport coordinates.
- **Attribution.** Radix Colors, Primer Primitives and Alabaster are MIT. Their copyright lines and the permission notice are in a `/*! … */` comment at the top of `index.css` (and of `syntax-palettes.ts` for Alabaster), which esbuild keeps in the minified bundle, and in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` with what was taken from each. Work that was drawn on without copying anything (GitHub's colorblind themes, JetBrains' grey deletions) is listed there too. A value copied from another work later gets the same two notices.
- **Linted.** `local/css-design-tokens` rejects color literals, absolute font sizes in `font`/`font-size`/`*font-size*` custom properties, and `--beuve-scale-*` reads in Lit `css` templates. `local/css-logical-properties` rejects physical properties and values and asymmetric shorthands there. Both apply to `src/ui`. `index.css` is the one place literals live.

## Consequences

Colors and sizes are picked from a short named list, so a component's styles say what each value is for. A theme change or a new scheme is an edit to `index.css` alone. Adding a color means adding its Radix steps (both themes) and a semantic token, never a literal in a component. Dense views show less at once: the sidebar is wider (`clamp(320px, 28vw, 420px)`) and the file tree, lists and status bar take more room. A layout with a fixed height sized for 11 px text may clip and needs its height in rem or removed. Strings handed to `@pierre/diffs` and `@pierre/trees` as CSS are outside the lint and are kept logical by hand. The research did not find a published source arguing that red/green diff colors carry a value judgement. That is our reasoning, not a citation.

## Amendment 2026-11-06: no literal lengths outside the token file

Every length in the renderer is a token now, not only font sizes. `index.css` adds border widths (`--beuve-border-width`, `-thick`, `-heavy`), `--beuve-space-px` and `--beuve-space-1-5`, `--beuve-radius-tiny`, `--beuve-letter-spacing-caps`, `--beuve-font-size-mark`, `--beuve-line-height-compact`, `--beuve-shadow-sheet`, and `--beuve-size-*` tokens. The size tokens are named by purpose: dots, icons and controls; fields, labels, editors and scroll boxes; menus, popovers, the reading column and dialogs; fractions of the window such as `--beuve-size-window-block-60`. A width sized to text is a count of `--beuve-measure-em` or `--beuve-measure-ch` (`calc(13 * var(--beuve-measure-em))`), so a column states how much text it holds and still grows with the font. Arbitrary values were rounded onto the scale (3px and 5px paddings to 4px and 6px, 10px dialog radii to 12px, the 30px tab bar to the 32px row). The traffic-light inset stays in pixels as a token, because macOS draws those buttons at a fixed size.

`local/css-design-tokens` rejects a length (`px`, `rem`, `em`, `vh`, `vw`, `ch`, the container and dynamic viewport units, and the absolute units) in a `css` template, and in any other string or template literal in `src/ui`: an `html` style attribute, CSS built as a string, a `setProperty` value. `0`, `%`, `fr` and unitless numbers stay allowed. A length computed at run time, such as a measured card height or the dragged sidebar width, is concatenated (`${String(height)}px`) and never matches. The one disabled line is the diff stream's `IntersectionObserver` `rootMargin`, an API that takes no `var()` and holds a virtualization distance (ADR 0025), not a design value. Durations (`120ms` transitions) are not lengths and are not covered.

## Amendment 2026-11-06: themes through `light-dark()`

Each colour token that differs by theme is now one `light-dark(light, dark)` pair, not a value in `:root` that a `prefers-color-scheme: dark` block overrides. `:root { color-scheme: light dark }` still follows the system, so the app is unchanged, but anything that sets `color-scheme` picks the theme: Storybook's toolbar does (ADR 0117), and a theme preference could later. Script that needs a resolved colour reads it back from a probe element's computed `color`, as the terminal panel does for xterm; `getPropertyValue` would return the `light-dark()` text.

## Amendment 2026-11-06: blue and yellow, the default scheme

A fourth scheme, `blue-yellow` ("Blue and yellow", listed first in Settings › Diff › Colors), is now the default: added lines blue, removed lines yellow. This is the pair this ADR named as a candidate, from Review Board and Bitbucket. Like `blue-orange`, it is distinguishable under the common colour vision deficiencies, and neither hue reads as an error or a success. Unlike `blue-orange`, its removed side is far from both the error red and the pink of comments (ADR 0119).

- **Steps.** Added uses blue a5/a6/a7, as `blue-orange` does. Removed uses yellow a4/a5/a6, with yellow 9 bars and yellow 11 counts. Radix's yellow alpha steps are strong in light and faint in dark. a3/a4/a5 balanced with blue in light but nearly vanished in dark; a5/a6/a7 suited dark but was heavy in light. a4/a5/a6, the steps `blue-orange` uses for orange, reads at a similar weight to blue in both.
- **Default.** The `:root` block holds the `blue-yellow` tokens, `red-green` moves to its own `[data-diff-colors]` selector like the others, and main, the IPC schema, `applyDiffColors`, the settings view and Storybook default to `blue-yellow`.
- **Existing settings.** `display:set` writes every display field whenever one changes. So a reviewer who changed any display setting before this has `diffColors: 'red-green'` saved, chosen or not, and keeps red and green. They cannot be told apart from reviewers who chose it, so nothing is migrated. Fresh installs and untouched settings get blue and yellow.

## Amendment 2026-11-06: one diff color scheme, changed by editing the source

beuve is a home-cooked tool, configured the way suckless tools are: by editing the source and rebuilding, not through settings. The diff colors were the one appearance setting, so they go. Blue added and yellow removed is the only scheme.

- **Removed.** Settings › Diff › Colors, the `diffColors` field of `display:get`/`display:set` and its schema, `applyDiffColors`, the `[data-diff-colors]` blocks in `index.css`, and the Storybook toolbar's scheme switch. The `--beuve-diff-*` tokens in `:root` are the scheme. The Radix steps only the other schemes used (green and red a5–a7, all of orange) stay, so switching back is an edit to the diff tokens alone. `index.css` now keeps a scale's steps whether or not anything reads them, rather than listing only the steps in use.
- **Changing the colors.** Edit the `--beuve-diff-*` tokens in `src/ui/index.css`, adding the Radix steps they need, and rebuild. The removed schemes' token blocks are in git history, and the steps above stay a reasonable guide: line < gutter number < changed word, one alpha step apart, bars step 9, counts step 11.
- **Existing settings.** A `diffColors` key in an older `settings.json` is no longer read. It stays in the file, like any key beuve does not know, and does nothing. A reviewer who had red and green, or another scheme, now sees blue and yellow.
- **The general rule** is [ADR 0122](0122-themes-are-edited-in-the-source.md): themes are edited in the source, fonts may be settings.

## Amendment (2026-11-10): the diff's own blending is off; lighter line tints

`@pierre/diffs` blends a changed line's and gutter number's colour into the background (`--mix-light: 88%`, `--mix-dark: 80%`, so 12% and 20% of the token) but paints a changed word's colour as given. The `--beuve-diff-*` steps (line < number < word) therefore showed as a near-invisible line under a full-strength word: changed lines were hard to tell from unchanged ones, and changed words looked too dark. `beuve-file-card` now adopts a one-rule style sheet into the diff's shadow root that sets both mixes to 0%, so the tokens are the colours shown, and the tokens were retuned against the running app: a changed line is 30% of blue a5 (yellow a4), its number 45% of blue a6 (yellow a5), and a changed word blue a5 (yellow a4), painted over its line. Blue a7 and yellow a6 are no longer used by the diff. Other users of the line tokens (the patch preview, the flow graph, the plain fallback) get the lighter line tint too.
